Two directions for the rebuild: scroller + chart + subtle co-pilot

Building on the Optio Employee Portal design system. Both concepts keep the existing scroller and the per-year/per-plan/per-instrument filters. The difference is where the chart sits, how the user filters on it, and how the AI co-pilot gets a job that doesn't take the whole screen.

Concept A

Chart ↔ Table, chat as a sticky co-pilot bar

Keeps the current layout intact. The scroller and summary cards stay on top. Below them, a Chart/Table toggle replaces the single table. The AI co-pilot lives as a slim bar pinned to the bottom of the viewport — always within reach, never a modal takeover.

What it does

  1. Scroller + summary cards sit exactly where they do today — zero relearning for existing users.
  2. One Chart/Table toggle replaces the "Show graph" button. Default view becomes the chart because most questions are temporal.
  3. Per-year / Per-plan / Per-instrument tabs keep the existing mental model but now reshape the chart series, not just the table.
  4. The co-pilot bar lives at the viewport bottom, always visible but visually small (one line). Click to expand; the chart stays in view behind a translucent panel.
  5. Table is still available as a collapsible section below the chart — keeps spreadsheet-brain users happy.
Trade-off Lowest friction for people who already know the old simulator. The co-pilot does not become part of the visual hierarchy — risk is it reads as "optional support" rather than the main power unlock.

Concept B

Chart-first. Scroller on the timeline. Chat as a lens.

The chart becomes the main surface. The scroller is reshaped to sit directly on the chart's year axis — dragging it animates the simulated line and moves a vertical reference line. The AI co-pilot isn't a separate chat — it's a row of suggested-question chips that, when tapped, highlight parts of the chart and surface a short inline answer. The table collapses into a drawer underneath.

What it does

  1. Chart is the main surface. Current vs simulated are one picture, not a pair of number cards.
  2. The scroller is on the axis. Dragging it = dragging a point in time, which is what the user is actually doing.
  3. Filters sit above the chart as inline pills — they reshape what's drawn, not just a table underneath.
  4. The co-pilot shows up as suggested-question chips under the chart. Tapping one highlights part of the chart and drops a one-sentence answer in-place. No separate chat screen, no modal.
  5. The table becomes a drawer. It's for people who need to export or triple-check the numbers — not the first thing anyone sees.
Trade-off Bigger visual shift from today's simulator — existing users need one short onboarding pass. In return, the AI co-pilot is woven into the actual data surface instead of parked next to it.

Which direction next?

Concept A ships faster and asks nothing new of existing users. Concept B treats the AI as part of the data surface and bets on a short learning curve. Happy to push either toward mid-fi — just say which.
