01 · The monthly loop

One missed value held up the whole month.

Finance teams at our client companies had to file a report on their share programs every month. The numbers were in the product, but the report was built somewhere else:

  1. Export the data
  2. Paste it into Excel
  3. Apply the rules by hand
  4. Send the file out

↺Something changed? Customer Success rebuilt the spreadsheet. Again.

The rule I designed against

If a screen asks a finance team to do work the system could do, the design isn’t finished.

02 · Mapping the month

Before drawing a screen, I drew the whole month.

Who gets the data, who checks it, who builds the file, who waits. Laying it out in lanes showed where people were doing the system’s job. Then I turned each part into a user story, screen by screen.

I didn’t do it from my desk. Clients tested and pushed back the whole way through, and their signals were coded across hundreds of sessions.

03 · Four decisions

Four decisions that moved the work out of Excel.

The core

A guided report flow inside the product

WasA file built by handNowPick the period, the rest fills in

Values, rules and breakdowns come from the system. The same place handles every report type the team has to file.

Built unprompted

Grouping you set up once

WasRegrouped in Excel every monthNowEvery future report inherits it

Each client groups their report differently. Nobody asked for this, but it removed about 1 000 hours a year of manual Excel work.

Strategic

Currency rules in the product

WasRates and country overrides fixed by handNowChange a rate without a spreadsheet

Only the right people can change it. Every change is checked, and the defaults are safe.

Problem reframed

Fair value, guided

WasHire an outside expertNowAdmins run it and trust the result

Explanations sit next to each field, the formulas are visible, and you can compare with earlier runs.

04 · Where AI helped

AI didn’t replace the finance experts. It got me to them faster.

Prototype

A working flow, not frames

I built the new currency-rate setup in v0 and walked into the engineering review with something that ran. See the v0 chat ↗

Words

A first draft of the help text

AI drafted tooltips and error messages in finance language. A finance expert and I edited every line before it shipped.

Second opinion

“What would an auditor say?”

Before a real review, I asked AI to object to a flow. Two rounds out of three were noise. The third caught a real hole.

Said no to

AI-drawn screens. Here, one wrongly named field can break a report. A wrong label costs more than AI saves by drawing it.

05 · What I’d change

I shipped cards where people needed a table.

The reports list could have been a table: sortable columns, dense rows, the format finance admins live in. I chose cards, because cards matched the rest of the product.

What I’d do now

Keep the table, accept the break from the design system, and write down why. Power users needed to sort, scan and act more than they needed things to match.
