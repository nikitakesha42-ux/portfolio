Problem

Optio's identity had grown organically across product, sales and marketing — and it showed. The brand felt fragmented, the product looked dated, and the visual story didn't match where the company was heading.

How I framed it

A brand refresh can easily turn into a moodboard exercise. To avoid that, I treated it as a product problem first: the brand exists to make the product easier to recognise, easier to trust, and easier to talk about. That meant starting with three questions — what does Optio actually stand for, who is it for, and what should it feel like compared to the alternatives? — and only opening Figma once the answers were clear enough to be defended.

Goal

  • Refresh Optio toward a modern, friendly, trustworthy product feel.
  • Build one visual system that works across product, marketing and sales surfaces.
  • Document brand decisions so non-designers can stay on-brand without guessing.

02Core Challenges

  • Years of inconsistent brand expression had left every team improvising — slides, decks, UI and emails all looked like different companies.
  • The product carried heavy financial content but felt cold and dated, which made the first impression harder than it needed to be.
  • Stakeholders had strong opinions but no shared rationale — brand discussions kept stalling in subjective debates.

03Key Solutions

Strategy first, visuals second

I started with working sessions with the founders and marketing to align on what Optio stands for, who it serves and how it should feel — and only then opened Figma.

A modern, warmer visual identity

The previous identity leaned on heavy corporate fintech blue — appropriate for a calculator vendor, but the wrong signal for a product company that wants people to feel safe interacting with their own equity. The new palette stays trustworthy but breathes more, the typography carries more confidence than the old wordmark allowed, and the 3D illustrations were chosen because they make abstract financial concepts feel tangible without becoming cartoonish. Every choice was tested against one question: would a finance lead, an HR partner and a regular employee all read this as serious and approachable?

Tokenised, scalable design system

A brand only holds together if the people building day-to-day work don't have to think about it. I rebuilt the identity into design tokens (colour, type, spacing, elevation) and shipped them to engineering, so a brand decision made once propagates across the product instead of having to be re-applied screen by screen. New surfaces inherit the brand by default; new contributors don't need a brand book to stay on-brand. That was the deliberate goal — make the system the path of least resistance.

04Brand System

The system covered product UI, marketing site, pitch decks, sales collateral, and onboarding — all built from the same primitives so any team could compose new material without going off-brand.

  • Colour palette and semantic tokens for every surface.
  • Type scale that worked from product UI to marketing headlines.
  • Illustration direction and component patterns shared across teams.
  • Brand guidelines documented for non-designers — so marketing and sales could ship independently.

05Collaboration

I worked closely with the founders to align on direction, with marketing to land the launch, and with engineering to bring the system into the product. The role was as much facilitator as designer — helping the team agree on a shared definition of the brand.

06Outcomes

  • A more unified visual identity across product, marketing and sales — the same Optio wherever a customer meets it.
  • Sales and marketing felt the brand was helping rather than getting in the way.
  • Set the design tokens and visual language that the Employee Portal and Financial Reporting redesigns later built on.
