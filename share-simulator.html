01 · The problem

The old simulator answered a question people weren’t asking.

At Optio, I was one of the designers on this page. You moved a slider to pick a price, like “five times today’s”, and read two totals. It was correct, and honest about being a guess. It was made for someone fluent in finance.

So people found their own ways:

  • 📸Screenshotsto compare with next time
  • 🧮Excelto redo the maths in their own money
  • ✉️Emails to HRand then waiting for an answer

A simulator that only returns a number isn’t simulating much.

02 · What people needed

People wanted their own reality, not the programme’s.

I started with what people did when the simulator let them down. One need sat under all the others: your currency, your tax, what actually lands in your bank account.

My research wall: behaviours, clusters, journeys and How Might We. Tap to open full size.

The question I kept asking

How do I show people their own reality, so they feel informed instead of guessing?

Three rules for every screen

  1. Lead with what you’d receive.Money after tax, in your currency, first. Fine print last.
  2. Say what you can do.“Can sell”, “Soon” and “Later” instead of “vested” and “unvested”.
  3. Show what changed.A small marker on what moved since your last visit, so nobody needs a screenshot.

Three tries, one lesson: show the answer first.

Every early idea made people do something before they saw their own number.

Filters first

04 · Phone first

I started on the smallest screen, because that’s where people ask.

Priya checks her shares at night on her phone. A small screen won’t let you put off hard choices: one question per screen, one main button, every number in words.

The phone wireframes. Every screen had to earn its place here first.

05 · Finding the balance

First too much. Then too little. Then the middle.

When I cut too hard, today’s price and its source disappeared. The amount became a number nobody could check. And a number you can’t check is a number you don’t trust.

From a slider to a sentence

The old page made you think in multiples of the price. The new one names the choice in words and answers with money.

Words people already use

No blackout risk→No waiting, no company sale pause
already vested→already yours to sell
06 · The AI co-pilot

The co-pilot explains the maths. The decision stays with you.

What it does

The maths runs itself

Tax, unlock dates and sale pauses come from your own shares, so no two numbers disagree.

Where it sits

Next to the number

The answer opens the page. The co-pilot waits one tap away: “Why?”

What it never does

Never “sell now”

It explains, compares and points to a tax advisor. Wrong advice about someone’s money isn’t worth the risk.

What it answers

Your own shares

“What if I leave?” gets an answer with your numbers.

What if I leave in 18 months?

How is Norwegian tax different from UK?
co-pilot · compares, never advisesNorway taxes each share when it unlocks. The UK taxes when you sell. Same shares, very different timing.
07 · Built for everyone

The accessible way is the only way.

Ola uses VoiceOver. Priya holds the phone in one hand. There’s no special mode, just one version that works for both.

Screen readers

A chart you can hear

Arrow keys read out each year. “Describe this chart” tells the story in one sentence.

Colour

Never colour alone

Every status is a dot plus a word: “Can sell”, “Soon”, “Later”.

Touch

Made for one thumb

Big buttons, no surprise zoom, and it still works with the phone on its side.

Plain language

One tap to the meaning

Where a word has to stay, like “sale pause”, its meaning is one tap away.

08 · Looking ahead

It works, but no real user has tried it yet.

Next, I’d sit with people like Maren, Daniel, Priya and Ola, including someone who uses VoiceOver, and watch them find their answer. Here’s how I’d know it’s right:

Sign it works

Fewer “can I sell?” emails

People find the answer on the page instead of waiting for HR.

Sign it works

A quick first step

People pick a price or open a row soon after they arrive.

Sign it works

One visit is enough

Nobody asks “did this change since last time?”

What I’d tell myself at the start

Lesson

Simplify toward the question, not away from the facts

When the price and its source went, the trust went with them.

Lesson

Words are part of the design

Swapping “blackout” for “sale pause” was one word. It made the rule clear to everyone.

Lesson

Start small and the big screen gets easy

Every hard choice happened on the phone. The laptop only rearranged pieces that had earned their place.
